# v0.5 T59 — Stochastic Physical Substrate Selection Audit

**Status:** EVIDENCE-BASED SUBSTRATE AUDIT / NO PHYSICAL DEVICE TESTED  
**Parents:** T53–T58.  
**Purpose:** select experimental substrate classes capable of producing genuinely physical stochastic link-formation events without software implementing the q rule.

## 1. Required properties

A substrate suitable for a future T53 confirmation must provide:

1. **objective physical junctions** that are identifiable independently of graph statistics;
2. **stochastic physical switching latency or event probability** under a frozen drive;
3. **individual event readout** so that k_u and k_w can be estimated;
4. **low enough voltage/current** for controlled repeated measurements;
5. **reset or repeatability** for hazard estimation;
6. ability to construct equal-degree / distinct-q conditions;
7. a conventional material/electrical comparator rich enough to model known field, temperature, history and geometry effects;
8. topology changes that do not themselves dominate the candidate-junction physics;
9. no controller that computes q and chooses the winning junction.

Passing all nine remains necessary but not sufficient for a new-physics experiment.

## 2. Candidate A — Ag:SiO2 diffusive memristor crosspoints

Published Pt/Ag/Ag:SiO2/Pt diffusive devices show volatile, stochastic threshold switching. A representative study reports:

- device crosspoint scale 5 micrometers by 5 micrometers;
- switching around approximately 0.5 V;
- stochastic switching delay under repeated pulses;
- delay distributions measured from approximately 0.4 to 0.9 V;
- spontaneous return to the high-resistance state after removing/reducing drive;
- explicit dependence of delay statistics on voltage and pulse history/temperature.

This is close to the T53 observable: a repeated, naturally stochastic physical turn-on event with a measurable latency.

**Strengths**
- event can be assigned to one fabricated junction;
- stochastic delay is directly measurable;
- volatile reset permits repeated trials;
- low-voltage operation;
- conventional voltage/temperature dependencies are already recognized and can be included in k0.

**Limitations**
- the published device stack is microfabricated;
- reproducible junction arrays require thin-film deposition and lithography or collaboration/source devices;
- physical topology changes must be designed so that local field and temperature stay independently characterized.

**T59 classification:** BEST CURRENT CONFIRMATORY CLASS IF SUITABLE DEVICES CAN BE OBTAINED.

Primary evidence:
- Jiang et al., “A novel true random number generator based on a stochastic diffusive memristor,” Nature Communications (2017): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-00869-x

## 3. Candidate B — commercially available Ag-ion SDC memristors

Knowm currently documents packaged devices, research die and kits. Its public data sheet reports low quasi-static thresholds for several SDC variants, with typical forward thresholds around 0.26 V for W/Sn/C and around 0.33 V for Cr. The technology uses mobile Ag+ ions in chemically formed channels.

Knowm also publishes metastable-switch models in which switching probabilities depend on voltage and temperature.

**Strengths**
- presently obtainable as research hardware rather than requiring our own fabrication;
- low operating voltage;
- repeated electrical programming;
- individually addressable discrete devices;
- public device data and modeling resources.

**Limitations**
- SDC devices are engineered for comparatively stable/incremental memory behavior, not specifically the volatile stochastic-delay regime used in Candidate A;
- a probabilistic model does not prove that a given physical device/operating regime provides stationary spontaneous latency suitable for T53;
- before any q experiment, repeated fixed-pulse latency/probability distributions must be measured and stationarity/carryover tested.

**T59 classification:** BEST ACCESSIBLE CHARACTERIZATION CANDIDATE; NOT YET A CONFIRMATORY T53 SUBSTRATE.

Primary evidence:
- Knowm current technology/product page: https://knowm.org/memristors/
- Knowm data sheet: https://knowm.org/downloads/Knowm_Memristors.pdf
- Knowm metastable-switch model description: https://knowm.org/the-generalized-metastable-switch-memristor-model/

## 4. Candidate C — self-organizing Ag nanowire networks

Recent Ag nanowire networks display stochastic conductance fluctuations and jumps associated with formation/rupture of Ag conductive bridges at nanowire crosspoints. Published networks can potentiate under approximately 1 V constant stimulation and later relax.

**Strengths**
- genuinely networked and self-organizing physical substrate;
- relational structure is native rather than externally simulated;
- stochastic filament events arise from material dynamics.

**Limitations**
- assigning one observed network-level jump to one particular junction is difficult without spatially resolved instrumentation;
- network topology generally requires microscopy to reconstruct;
- changing q can redistribute electric fields throughout the same network, making the conventional k0 comparator difficult;
- exact equal-degree crossover control is substantially harder than with discrete crosspoints.

**T59 classification:** STRONG EXPLORATORY RELATIONAL-MATTER PLATFORM; WEAK FOR FIRST CLEAN T53 IDENTIFICATION.

Primary evidence:
- “Self-organizing neuromorphic nanowire networks as stochastic dynamical systems,” Nature Communications (2025): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-58741-2
- “Tomography of memory engrams in self-organizing nanowire connectomes,” Nature Communications (2023): https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-40939-x

## 5. Candidate D — macroscopic Cu electrochemical migration / dendrite bridging

Copper electrochemical migration in an electrolyte can produce stochastic dendrite growth and eventual conductive bridging. It is experimentally visible and much easier to construct at macroscopic scale.

**Strengths**
- low equipment barrier compared with nanofabrication;
- optical event observation is possible;
- actual conductive paths form physically rather than by software;
- useful for testing event-timing acquisition, imaging and competing-event analysis.

**Limitations**
- dendrite growth is already strongly controlled by conventional ion transport, electrochemistry, concentration depletion, field geometry and diffusion;
- real topology changes necessarily alter the electrochemical field and ionic environment;
- a condition-specific conventional k0 is difficult to establish tightly enough for a clean q residual;
- published demonstrations may use voltages around 10 V and micrometer-scale electrode gaps.

**T59 classification:** INSTRUMENTATION / HAZARD-PIPELINE REHEARSAL ONLY; NOT SELECTED FOR CONFIRMATORY NEW-PHYSICS TEST.

Primary evidence:
- “Numerical simulation of electrochemical migration of Cu based on the Nernst–Planck equation” (2021): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0017931021013673
- copper dendrite-growth experiments: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022072807002331

## 6. Candidate E — Ag2S / related custom ECM crossbar arrays

Ag2S memristive crossbars provide very low threshold voltages and stochastic filament-formation variability, including reported sub-0.3 V operation and device-to-device/cycle variation.

**Strengths**
- objective crosspoint cells;
- low voltage and strong filamentary interpretation;
- array geometry is well suited to repeated statistics.

**Limitations**
- custom wafer-scale fabrication;
- reported devices are commonly nonvolatile rather than automatically resetting;
- reset protocol and history become explicit parts of the hazard comparator.

**T59 classification:** STRONG CUSTOM-FAB ALTERNATIVE TO CANDIDATE A.

Primary evidence:
- “Wafer-Scale Ag2S-Based Memristive Crossbar Arrays with Ultra-Low Switching-Energies Reaching Biological Synapses” (2024): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11582288/

## 7. Selection by experimental role

### Role R1 — accessible next hardware characterization

**Selected class:** commercial discrete Ag-ion memristor hardware (Candidate B).

Purpose is not to test lambda_R yet. The first experiment asks only:

> Under a fixed, safely current-limited pulse protocol, does one physical device exhibit a reproducible stochastic switching-latency or switching-probability distribution after conditioning/reset, with enough stationarity to define a conventional hazard model?

If no, Candidate B is rejected for T53 and no q experiment follows.

### Role R2 — strongest clean confirmatory architecture

**Selected class:** individually addressable volatile diffusive Ag crosspoints (Candidate A), or a demonstrably equivalent volatile stochastic ECM device.

Reason: the relevant event itself — stochastic conductive-channel formation — is directly time-resolved, resettable, physically local and already known to depend on measurable conventional variables.

Availability/fabrication remains the present blocker.

### Role R3 — exploratory network physics

**Selected class:** Ag nanowire networks (Candidate C).

This is scientifically interesting for emergent relational dynamics but is not the first choice for identifying one q-dependent hazard coefficient.

### Role R4 — acquisition rehearsal

**Selected class:** macroscopic electrochemical dendrite system (Candidate D) only if a visible-event timing pipeline is needed before memristive hardware is available.

No result from R4 may be treated as a clean test of T53.

## 8. Critical scientific distinction

A stochastic device does not automatically test Resolutive physics.

For every selected substrate, ordinary stochastic filament physics is the null mechanism. A nonzero variation in threshold or latency is expected in these systems and is not anomalous by itself.

The future discriminator remains a **baseline-adjusted q dependence that transfers across held-out devices/crossovers after measured conventional variables are accounted for**.

## 9. Next protocol

T60 shall characterize one discrete physical memristor regime before constructing a relational network.

It must freeze:

- device type and serial/position identity;
- safe current compliance from the manufacturer's device limits;
- pulse amplitude and width;
- reset/conditioning sequence;
- switching threshold definition;
- repeated-trial count;
- latency censoring rule;
- temperature recording;
- stationarity/carryover criteria;
- conventional device-only hazard model;
- failure criterion.

No q contrast and no lambda_R estimation is authorized in T60.
