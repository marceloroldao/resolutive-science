## 2. Radius-one no-go

Consider a simple unweighted graph and a rooted, unlabeled **induced radius-one neighborhood in a tree**.

It is a star with d(v) leaves. Its isomorphism class is uniquely specified by d(v).

Consequently, any scalar F depending only on this rooted radius-one induced neighborhood and invariant under vertex relabeling must be of the form

F(v) = f(d(v)).

This statement does not apply to functions that additionally use vertex labels, edge weights, a background field, remote graph information, or an independently assigned physical state.

Therefore merely renaming d(v) as "resolutive density" or "resolutive energy" introduces no new information or physical dimension.

## 3. The exact local load identity

Define the graph wedge count

W(T) = sum_v binom(d(v),2).

A wedge is an unordered pair of incident edges sharing one center vertex; in a tree it corresponds to a unique simple path of length two.

When a new leaf x is attached to an existing node u of degree d before attachment:

- d(u) increases from d to d+1;
- d(x) becomes 1;
- all other degrees remain unchanged.

Therefore

Delta W = binom(d+1,2) - binom(d,2) + binom(1,2)
        = d.

Define the convex degree potential Phi(T)=sum_v d(v)^2. The same update yields

Delta Phi = (d+1)^2-d^2+1^2=2d+2.

Because, in a tree, sum_v d(v)=2(N-1),

Phi(T)=2W(T)+2(N-1).

Thus minimizing current parent degree among the *available frozen candidates* is **exactly equivalent** to minimizing the incremental wedge count Delta W and incremental Phi for that birth.

This is an exact mathematical identity, not a newly discovered law of nature.

## 4. Coordinate-free radius-two relation

Let S_2(v)={u : graph-distance(u,v)=2}.

In a tree, distinct nonbacktracking two-step paths have distinct endpoints at each root, so

|S_2(v)| = sum_{u adjacent to v}(d(u)-1),

and double-counting endpoint pairs gives

sum_v |S_2(v)| = 2 W(T).

Hence attaching a leaf to a degree-d parent gives

Delta [sum_v |S_2(v)|] = 2d.

The T25 MIN-degree rule is therefore a local greedy minimizer of the **immediate global count of two-step relational paths** among its available candidates.

## 5. Selection-pressure identity

Let C_t^* be the available candidates at a fixed birth event in a fixed current graph; write d_min=min_{u in C_t^*}d_t(u) and d_bar=mean_{u in C_t^*}d_t(u).

For a mixture that selects MIN-degree with probability alpha and a uniform available candidate otherwise,

E[Delta W | G_t,C_t^*]
  = alpha*d_min +(1-alpha)*d_bar.

For the same fixed state and candidate set the expectation is nonincreasing, and linear, in alpha:

d/dalpha E[Delta W | G_t,C_t^*] = d_min-d_bar <= 0.

If no sampled candidate is available, the fallback is independent of alpha under the T49 operator and does not change this conditional monotonic conclusion.

**Important:** this conditional identity does not prove that final W, M3 or M7 are globally monotonic after many births: later candidate degrees depend on earlier decisions. T49's full-chain dose response remains empirical within the model.

## 7. Next independently distinguishable graph quantity

The simplest strictly larger rooted neighborhood contains

L_2(v) = |S_2(v)| = sum_{u adjacent to v}(d(u)-1).

Two vertices may have the same d(v) but different L_2(v), so L_2 can distinguish local arrangements invisible to the radius-one state.

A dimensionless environmental load is, for d(v)>0,

ell_2(v) = L_2(v)/d(v).

It is an average *neighbor excess degree*.

This is a **candidate structural descriptor**, not an observed physical density or physical interaction energy.

A later test must first count admissible candidate sets containing equal-degree vertices with distinct L_2 values; without such discordance, L_2 cannot be identified independently of degree in the actual growth process.

No new selection law is authorized by T50.

## 8. Physical-dimension stop

The quantities d(v), W, L_2 and ell_2 are pure counts and are dimensionless.

There is no justified inference

graph distance = physical length,
birth index = physical time,
degree = physical energy,
W = physical energy,
alpha = physical coupling constant.

Assigning units after the fact would not remedy the absence of an independently specified physical mapping.

To form a physical constitutive law, one must specify a measurable state (e.g., voltage and conductance in a physically realized network), its transformation/dimension, and an observable with a prediction that differs from conventional theory without fit-to-target.

## 9. Minimal operational calibration — ordinary resistor network

For a **separately defined physical realization** with edges of conductance g_e (siemens) and node potentials V_i (volts),

I_e = g_e(V_i-V_j)

and the edge power is

P_e = g_e(V_i-V_j)^2   [watts].

One possible nodal power allocation is

P_i = (1/2) sum_{e incident to i} P_e  [watts].

Here P_i depends on conductances, applied boundary conditions and potentials, not merely on d(i). It is a physical observable of a *specified classical resistor network*, not an identification of W with energy.

For terminal nodes a,b, ordinary linear circuit theory predicts

R_eff(a,b)=(e_a-e_b)^T L_g^+ (e_a-e_b)  [ohms],

where L_g is the conductance-weighted graph Laplacian and + denotes the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse.

Any measured topology dependence of R_eff matching this expression is **ordinary circuit physics**, not evidence for a new force.

An independently derived Resolutive correction, with units, magnitude, sign, controls and failure criterion, is required before a resistor network can discriminate a new physical theory.
