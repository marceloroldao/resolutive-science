## Why finite-range symmetric rules tend to z=2

For a symmetric finite-range operator

```text
lambda(k)=sum_r 2 w_r [cos(rk)-1],
```

Taylor expansion gives

```text
lambda(k) = -k^2 sum_r w_r r^2 + O(k^4).
```

If the second moment is finite and nonzero, the leading exponent is therefore `z=2`.

This explains the robustness observed in T20 without elevating 2 to a universal Resolutive constant.

## How z changes

- An antisymmetric transport symbol `sin(k)` has leading order `k`, hence `z=1`.
- Squaring a Laplacian suppresses the quadratic term and gives leading `k^4`, hence `z=4`.
- Long-range power-law kernels can produce fractional low-k exponents in the infinite-range limit; finite-cutoff simulations are diagnostic only and are not frozen here as precision exponent measurements.
