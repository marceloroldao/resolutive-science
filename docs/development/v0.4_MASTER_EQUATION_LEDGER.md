## 1. State-space fork that must be resolved

There are currently at least two mathematically distinct interpretations of the half-angle representation.

### Interpretation A — ordinary phase variable with redundant cover

Let `phi = Theta/2` and identify `phi ~ phi + 2pi`. Then `Theta ~ Theta + 4pi` follows only because `Theta` is twice the ordinary phase coordinate. In this case `4pi` may be a coordinate-covering artifact rather than new physics.

Classification: **possible reparametrization**.

### Interpretation B — physical double cover

Treat states separated by `Theta -> Theta + 2pi` as physically distinct while `Theta -> Theta + 4pi` closes the state. Then an additional physical structure is required to distinguish `Psi` from `-Psi` or otherwise encode the double cover.

Classification: **postulate candidate**, not derived.

Required mathematical object may be, for example, a nontrivial bundle, covering space, discrete internal label, transition holonomy, or another explicitly defined structure. Naming such an object is not sufficient; its transformation law and observable effect must be specified.

## 2. Transformation audit

From the candidate representation,

```text
Psi(Theta + 2pi) = -Psi(Theta)
Psi(Theta + 4pi) =  Psi(Theta)
```

This algebraic fact is **derived from the parametrization**.

What is **not** derived is whether `Psi` and `-Psi` represent distinct physical states. If all observables are invariant under `Psi -> -Psi`, the apparent `4pi` closure has no direct physical consequence.

Therefore the immediate #85 test is:

> Find at least one admissible observable `O` for which `O[Psi] != O[-Psi]`, or prove that the state-space construction itself makes the two states inequivalent.

If neither can be done, `4pi` must not be advertised as a derived physical periodicity.

## 4. Minimal equivalence test

Before deriving phenomenology, compare the candidate `(rho, Theta)` sector with an ordinary complex scalar written in polar variables.

Starting from

```text
Psi = sqrt(rho) exp(i Theta/2)
```

a conventional kinetic term `|partial Psi|^2` expands algebraically into terms proportional to

```text
(partial rho)^2/(4 rho) + rho (partial Theta)^2/4.
```

Therefore ME-09 and ME-10 may arise from a standard complex-scalar kinetic term under the half-angle coordinate choice. This is an important equivalence warning: those two terms alone do not establish novel Resolutive dynamics.

Classification: **D algebraic equivalence candidate**, subject to metric/sign convention.
