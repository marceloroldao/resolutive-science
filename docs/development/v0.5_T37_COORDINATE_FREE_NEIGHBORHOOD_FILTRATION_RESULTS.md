## 1. Executive result

The scale-extension effect survives after removing profile coordinates and Euclidean profile geometry entirely.

Working directly with the nested graph-distance neighborhoods

```text
B_R(v) subseteq B_2R(v)
```

shows that doubling relational radius adds a strictly positive median fraction of new nodules for every frozen N and R comparison.

At the same time, adjacent nodules become progressively more similar in their finite-radius neighborhoods as R increases, as measured by a coordinate-free Jaccard set contrast.

Thus the branch now has a representation-independent structural result:

> Larger relational neighborhoods contain substantial new set information even while the neighborhoods of adjacent nodules overlap increasingly strongly.

This is a property of the frozen graph ensemble and filtration. It is not yet a physical metric, spatial dimension, or dynamical law.

## 4. Size behavior and finite-size occupancy

The 1->2 and 2->4 innovation distributions are nearly unchanged across N. The 4->8 median innovation is approximately 0.65-0.67 for all sizes.

Mean occupancy at R=8 is:

```text
N=128 : 0.2659
N=256 : 0.1521
N=512 : 0.0784
```

with medians 0.2344, 0.1289 and 0.0625 respectively.

Thus the 4->8 result is not explained by R=8 neighborhoods saturating the entire graph in the larger-N runs. No infinite-size limit is claimed.
