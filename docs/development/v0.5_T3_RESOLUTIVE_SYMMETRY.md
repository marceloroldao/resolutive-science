## 1. Purpose

T3 defines symmetry at the level actually justified by the v0.5 ontology. It does not assume spacetime, coordinates, a metric, differentiability, a Lie group, an action functional, or continuous parameters.

The objective is to separate:

1. structural/categorical symmetry;
2. discrete algebraic symmetry;
3. represented physical symmetry;
4. continuous dynamical symmetry;
5. Noether-type symmetry.

Only the first levels are currently available.

## 2. Structural Resolutive symmetry

Let `C_R` be the category-like Resolutive structure from T1.

A strict structural symmetry candidate is an invertible structure-preserving map

```text
S : C_R -> C_R
```

such that

```text
S(I_A) = I_{S(A)}
S(Gamma_2 o Gamma_1)
 = S(Gamma_2) o S(Gamma_1).
```

If `S` has a strict inverse functor, it is an automorphism of `C_R`.

More generally, categorical equivalence may replace strict equality when only structure up to isomorphism matters. T3 keeps these notions separate.

## 3. Resolutive automorphism group

The strict automorphisms of `C_R`, when set-theoretically well behaved, compose into

```text
Aut(C_R).
```

This is a symmetry group of the abstract relational structure.

Important:

```text
Aut(C_R) != physical symmetry group automatically.
```

A mathematical relabeling can preserve category structure without corresponding to a physically realizable transformation.

## 4. Physical equivalence compatibility

A candidate physical symmetry must preserve the equivalence structure used to define physical states and trajectory classes.

If

```text
A ~ B
```

or

```text
Gamma_1 ~_C Gamma_2,
```

then an admissible symmetry must satisfy the corresponding transformed equivalence.

Thus symmetry must descend consistently to physical quotient structure.

```text
T3_QUOTIENT_COMPATIBILITY = REQUIRED
```

## 5. Symmetry of a representation

Let a future observable representation be

```text
F : C_R -> C_obs.
```

A structural transformation `S` is physically invisible under `F` when the represented predictions are equivalent:

```text
F o S ~= F
```

where `~=` denotes the appropriate natural/observational equivalence in `C_obs`.

This provides a representation-relative notion of physical symmetry without yet assuming numbers.

## 6. Resolutive invariant

Given a symmetry family/group `G` acting on a set/object `X`, an invariant is any map/object `I` satisfying

```text
I(g . x) = I(x)
```

for all admissible `g`.

At the current level, invariants may be nonnumerical. Examples include:

- orbit membership;
- stabilizer type;
- isomorphism class;
- conjugacy class under suitable actions;
- composability/incidence relations;
- algebraic order where preserved.

Thus:

```text
SYMMETRY_INVARIANT != CONSERVED_QUANTITY
```

## 7. Orbits and stabilizers

For a group `G` acting on Resolutive states or loops, define

```text
Orb_G(x) = {g.x | g in G}
```

and

```text
Stab_G(x) = {g in G | g.x = x}.
```

Orbit structure classifies what the symmetry can transform into what. Stabilizers encode transformations leaving a particular object fixed.

These are available for discrete groups and require no continuity.

## 8. Symmetry action on loop groups

For a state `A`, a symmetry mapping `A` to `S(A)` induces, when well-defined,

```text
Aut_R(A) -> Aut_R(S(A)).
```

If `S(A)=A`, it acts on the loop group at the same state.

A structure-preserving symmetry must preserve group-theoretic relations such as identity, inverses, products, algebraic order and commutation relations.

Hence if

```text
[alpha,beta] = e_A,
```

then their symmetry images must also commute.

## 9. Symmetry does not yet imply conservation

A conserved quantity requires a notion of evolution or comparison along a parameterized process plus a quantity whose value is preserved along that evolution.

T1-T3 do not yet supply:

```text
continuous parameter lambda
derivative d/dlambda
action functional
dynamical equations
infinitesimal generators
```

Therefore:

```text
T3_NOETHER_CONSERVATION = NOT_AVAILABLE
```

No conservation law may be claimed merely from categorical symmetry.

## 10. What is required for Noether-type reasoning

A standard continuous Noether route requires at least:

1. a continuous/differentiable configuration or state space;
2. parameterized histories/fields;
3. a differentiable action functional `S[Phi]` or suitable variational dynamics;
4. a continuous one-parameter transformation group;
5. invariance of the action up to the allowed boundary term;
6. equations of motion/Euler-Lagrange structure;
7. regularity conditions sufficient to derive a current/charge.

None of these follows from category structure alone.

## 11. Minimal continuous symmetry bridge

The weakest possible bridge toward continuous symmetry is to introduce a one-parameter family

```text
S_lambda
```

with

```text
S_0 = Id
S_lambda o S_mu = S_{lambda+mu}
```

plus an appropriate topology/continuity condition.

Only with differentiability could one define an infinitesimal generator

```text
X = dS_lambda/dlambda |_(lambda=0).
```

T3 does not introduce this bridge; it only records it as an explicit future assumption.

## 12. Discrete symmetry can still constrain theory

Even without Noether, discrete/categorical symmetry can rule out candidate laws or representations.

If a proposed observable `Q` is claimed to be symmetry-invariant, then

```text
Q(S(Gamma)) = Q(Gamma)
```

must hold.

If it fails, either:

- `Q` is not an invariant;
- `S` is not a physical symmetry;
- or the representation breaks the symmetry.

Thus symmetry already provides falsifiable internal consistency conditions for later model construction.

## 13. Symmetry breaking — terminology boundary

At the current level, one may define only a structural distinction:

```text
law/structure admits S
but a selected object/state is not fixed by S.
```

This is analogous to a symmetry action with a nontrivial orbit, but T3 does not yet invoke spontaneous symmetry breaking, vacuum states, order parameters or phase transitions.

Those require additional dynamical/continuous structure.

## 14. Relation to T2 noncommutativity

A symmetry automorphism preserves the commutator structure of invertible loops:

```text
S([alpha,beta])
 = [S(alpha),S(beta)].
```

Therefore the distinction between commuting and noncommuting loop histories is structural under admissible automorphisms.

This strengthens T2: noncommutativity is not merely an artifact of a chosen naming of loops if the transformation is a genuine algebra automorphism.

## 15. First invariant hierarchy

The v0.5 theory can now distinguish:

```text
LEVEL I0 — identity/incidence invariants
LEVEL I1 — algebraic invariants of loops
LEVEL I2 — invariants under categorical/discrete symmetry
LEVEL I3 — representation-relative physical invariants
LEVEL I4 — continuous symmetry invariants
LEVEL I5 — dynamical conserved quantities / Noether charges
```

Only I0-I2 are structurally available now. I3 requires a physical representation; I4-I5 require additional continuous/dynamical structure.
