## Question

Why did d_g, d_s and d_w disagree for the first coordinate-free endogenous network?

## 1. Graph class implied by the frozen generator

The T25 generator adds approximately one successful edge for each new nodule. Consequently E is approximately N-1 and mean degree tends to 2.

This is the signature of a sparse tree or near-tree. The generator does not create an extensive density of independent cycles.

Therefore the first candidate is not a generic finite-dimensional lattice analogue. It belongs to a sparse branching-network class.

## 2. Growth-dimension failure mode

A finite-dimensional polynomial-growth geometry requires a stable window

V(r) ~ r^d.

Trees with persistent branching instead tend toward exponential or non-polynomial volume growth before finite-size saturation. Fitting a power law over a short finite window can therefore return an apparent d_g that drifts with graph size.

The observed upward drift of d_g with N is consistent with this failure mode.

Classification:
T26_GROWTH_POWER_LAW_WINDOW = NOT_ESTABLISHED.

## 3. Spectral estimator caveat

The T25 spectral fit used even return times 2..12. On sparse tree-like graphs this short window is vulnerable to:

- local degree structure;
- bipartite/parity effects;
- pre-asymptotic behavior;
- finite graph mixing;
- localized/branching modes.

The numerical stability of d_s near ~0.93 is therefore insufficient to call it an asymptotic spectral dimension.

Classification:
T26_DS_SHORT_WINDOW = DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY.

## 4. Walk estimator caveat

The walk fit also used short times. On a heterogeneous branching graph, graph-distance mean-square displacement can cross between local, branching and finite-size regimes.

The measured d_w variation is therefore not evidence of a universal walk exponent.

Classification:
T26_DW_STABLE_SCALING = NOT_ESTABLISHED.

## 5. Cross-estimator relation

For many regular fractal diffusion settings one may encounter relations such as

d_s = 2 d_f / d_w,

but this requires assumptions about scaling regimes and diffusion. T26 does not impose this identity.

Using unstable finite-window exponents to enforce the relation would be circular.

## 6. Local versus global dimension

A network can possess local neighborhoods with one effective growth rate and global neighborhoods with another. T26 therefore requires a future radius-dependent diagnostic

d_eff(r) = d log V(r) / d log r

rather than reducing each graph immediately to one scalar d_g.

Likewise define a running spectral dimension

d_s(t) = -2 d log P_return(t) / d log t.

Plateaus, not isolated fitted values, are the target evidence.

## 7. Coarse-graining criterion

A credible emergent dimension must survive a declared graph coarse-graining operation. T26 does not yet select that operation because arbitrary graph contraction can manufacture dimensional behavior.

The future coarse-graining rule must be specified independently of the desired exponent.

## 8. What T25 actually established

T25 did establish that a coordinate-free relational graph can be generated and interrogated without assigning Euclidean dimension.

It did not establish that every coordinate-free relational graph has a useful finite effective dimension.

This distinction is scientifically important.

The negative T25 result narrows the search. Coordinate freedom alone is insufficient. To obtain a continuum-like finite-dimensional geometry, the relational dynamics must additionally generate the appropriate large-scale volume/spectral structure.

That requirement must arise from an independent dynamical principle rather than from demanding three dimensions.
