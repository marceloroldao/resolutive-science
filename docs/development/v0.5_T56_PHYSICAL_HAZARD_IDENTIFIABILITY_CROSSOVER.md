# v0.5 T56 — Physical Hazard Identifiability and Crossover Gate

**Status:** ANALYTICAL IDENTIFIABILITY AUDIT; PHYSICAL EXPERIMENT NOT EXECUTED  
**Parent:** T55 and exploratory T53.  
**Scope:** define what must be measured independently for a spontaneous-link experiment to distinguish the proposed q term from ordinary device physics.

## 1. T55 is necessary but insufficient

T55 constructs candidate nodes with equal graph degree (1), q=(1,2,3), and equal ideal DC resistance (2000 ohm) from the same reference terminal.

Equal DC port response does not fix the physical *formation hazards* of microscopic candidate junctions.

The conventional event hazards may depend on fields, temperature, geometry, wetting, interfaces, materials, stochastic disorder and history; those physical conditions may be changed by a real topology modification even when ordinary DC port resistance is unchanged.

No inference k_u^0=k_w^0 is licensed by the T55 equal-port result.

## 2. Explicit identifiability no-go

The T53 exploratory ansatz is

y = y_0 - lambda_R Delta q,

where

y = log(k_u/k_w),
y_0 = log(k_u^0/k_w^0).

Suppose only y and Delta q are observed, and y_0 is an unrestricted unknown.

For **any** chosen value lambda*, one can choose

y_0* = y + lambda* Delta q

to obtain exactly the same observed y.

Therefore lambda_R is structurally unidentifiable without a constraint or independent measurement of the conventional baseline y_0.

Even an unlimited number of event observations cannot fix this identification problem if the baseline can change freely with every condition.

This is a mathematical no-go for inference from event ratios alone, not a claim that the exploratory postulate is false.

## 3. Competing-event readout

For two independent, stationary exponential event hazards k_u and k_w, the probability that u forms first is

Pr(u first)=k_u/(k_u+k_w),

so the first-event odds are

Pr(u first)/Pr(w first)=k_u/k_w.

This identity requires the specified competing-risk assumptions and no informative censoring. Otherwise the time-resolved hazard model must be defined and preregistered.

The event detector may log outcomes but must not calculate q and use it to select which physical connection forms.

## 4. Baseline-adjusted contrast

Define, for condition c,

D_c = log[(k_u^c/k_w^c)/(k_u^{0,c}/k_w^{0,c})].

Here k^0 is an independently calibrated or independently predicted conventional physical hazard under **the same actual condition c**, including changes in geometry, field and temperature due to rewiring.

Under the exploratory T53 ansatz,

D_c=-lambda_R Delta q_c.

There must be no post-hoc fitting of the conventional comparator to absorb or manufacture the held-out effect.

## 5. Same-device crossover

Take the **same physical candidate junctions** u and w, with the same individual identities, in two predeclared conditions:

A: Delta q_A=+delta;
B: Delta q_B=-delta;

with delta=1 or 2.

The ideal model predicts

D_A=-lambda_R delta,
D_B=+lambda_R delta.

Therefore the antisymmetric and symmetric contrasts are

H_delta=(D_A-D_B)/(2 delta)=-lambda_R,

S_delta=(D_A+D_B)/2=0.

Fixed device bias cancels in an ideal balanced crossover. But physical rewiring itself may have conventional effects; these remain inside each independently determined k^{0,c}.

A schematic relabeling or resistor subdivision that does not create/remove objective physical links must not change measured q.

## 6. Shape test across held-out contrasts

If the same lambda_R applies to delta=1 and delta=2, then

H_1=H_2=-lambda_R.

Equivalently, the unnormalized antisymmetric contrast doubles:

(D_A-D_B) at delta=2
=
2 * [(D_A-D_B) at delta=1].

These equalities are parameter-independent shape predictions, but the conventional null lambda_R=0 also satisfies the zero case. To distinguish H1 from H0 a held-out nonzero directional effect is additionally required.

An absolute target effect size cannot be preregistered from T49, since T49 did not derive lambda_R.

## 7. Required conventional negatives and randomization

- Objective physical contact/junction identities are frozen prior to assigning q.
- Material stack, geometry, temperature, local electric field and power are independently characterized for each condition.
- Physical crossover order is randomized, with rest/washout and carryover checks appropriate to the material.
- Sham rewiring applies the same handling but leaves q unchanged.
- Negative controls test the conventional hazard comparator and its uncertainty before held-out event data are used.
- A programmable device choosing contacts according to q is excluded from the physical discovery sample.
- Distinct calibration and confirmation cohorts are required.
- A material-specific censoring and competing-risks model, sample size, statistical error budget and acceptance interval must be fixed before a confirmatory run.
