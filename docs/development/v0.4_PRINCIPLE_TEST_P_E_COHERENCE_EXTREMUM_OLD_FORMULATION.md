## 1. Existing RSMS status

The RSMS reserves a coherence operator but does not assign it a complete normative mathematical meaning at the foundational level. Its codomain, metric and physical interpretation require a later specialization.

Therefore the old formulation contains the *name and slot* for coherence, but not a unique scalar functional `C[R]` suitable for extremization.

## 2. Candidate functional freedom

Given the inherited state one could write many inequivalent candidates, for example

```text
C_1[R] = integral |grad R|^2,
C_2[R] = variance-like state functional,
C_3[R] = phase-alignment functional,
C_4[R] = entropy/information functional,
C_5[R] = distance from a reference state,
```

with many choices of internal metric, measure, normalization and weighting.

None is selected by the current RSMS primitive definitions.

## 3. Extremum direction is itself free

Even after selecting a functional, the old formulation does not determine whether physical evolution should:

- maximize coherence;
- minimize incoherence;
- conserve coherence;
- approach a stationary point;
- follow a gradient flow;
- obey a constrained extremum;
- balance coherence against another quantity.

These alternatives produce inequivalent physics.

## 4. Information-theoretic import test

Importing Shannon entropy, Fisher information, relative entropy, free energy or another standard functional would supply conventional mathematics but would not be a derivation from the RSMS primitives.

Such a choice could become a postulate, but it would have to be frozen with units, measure, reference distribution, operational meaning and a falsifiable observable before any target-data fit.

## 5. Does coherence close the Master Equation?

No.

Without a uniquely defined functional and extremization law, coherence acts as an interpretive label rather than a dynamical selector.
