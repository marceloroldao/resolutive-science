# v0.5 T55 — Equal-Degree / Distinct-Second-Shell Structural and Electrical Feasibility

**Status:** FROZEN BEFORE NUMERICAL EXECUTION; ANALYTICAL CONSTRUCTION  
**Parent:** T54. **Scope:** feasibility of the T53 q contrasts under a fully specified ordinary electrical comparator. Not a test of new physics.

## 1. Question

Can equal-degree candidate nodes with q=|S2|/d equal to 1, 2, and 3 be realized in a physically interpretable tree while having identical *ideal conventional DC resistance* to the same reference terminal?

This is a necessary but not sufficient prerequisite for a T53 hazard comparison.

## 2. Fixed graph

Vertices (exact labels):

r, A, B, C, U, V, W, B1, C1, C2.

Edges (exactly nine):

(r,A), (r,B), (r,C),
(A,U),
(B,V), (B,B1),
(C,W), (C,C1), (C,C2).

No other edges.

This is a connected tree with N=10 and maximum degree 4.

The three candidate nodes are U, V and W.

## 3. Structural gates

For a leaf u in a tree, its sole neighbor p has

d(u)=1 and q(u)=|S2(u)|=d(p)-1.

Thus the frozen graph predicts

d(U)=d(V)=d(W)=1,
d(A)=2, d(B)=3, d(C)=4,

q(U)=1, q(V)=2, q(W)=3.

With ordered pairs (V,U), (W,U):

Delta q=1 and Delta q=2.

These are discrete topology contrasts, not physical differences in energy.

## 4. Fixed conventional electrical model

Each of the nine edges is an independent ideal resistor of R=1000 ohm (g=0.001 S).

Terminals for each comparison are (r,U), (r,V) and (r,W), separately. For each measurement apply V_r=3.0 V and V_target=0.0 V, leaving every other node electrically floating and without external injection.

Solve Kirchhoff's current law at every floating node using the weighted graph Laplacian.

Do not fit resistances or use measured data to select the graph.

## 5. Exact standard prediction

A tree has a unique path between two terminals. Every edge off that path lies in a dangling subtree with no other terminal, so by KCL its current is zero.

The three reference-to-candidate paths are

r-A-U,
r-B-V,
r-C-W.

Each has two 1000-ohm series resistors. Therefore

R_eff(r,U)=R_eff(r,V)=R_eff(r,W)=2000 ohm.

At 3.0 V:

I=1.5 mA and P_total=4.5 mW

for each separately driven terminal pair.

All off-path branch currents are exactly zero in the ideal DC comparator.

This is conventional electrical physics, independent of any Resolutive postulate.

## 6. Frozen computational gates

The numerical script shall:

1. verify N=10, |E|=9, connectivity and tree class;
2. verify degree(U,V,W)=1 and q(U,V,W)=(1,2,3);
3. verify Delta q(V,U)=1, Delta q(W,U)=2;
4. construct the ordinary weighted Laplacian from nine 1000-ohm resistors;
5. solve the three Dirichlet boundary-value problems;
6. report equivalent R, terminal I and total dissipated power;
7. check KCL at floating nodes;
8. check off-path branch currents vanish within floating-point tolerance.

The static numerical consistency tolerance is 1e-9 relative for R, I, P and 1e-12 A absolute for KCL/off-path currents. These tolerances are numerical verification thresholds, not physical laboratory tolerances.

## 7. Explicit limitations

Equal DC port resistance does **not** establish equal stochastic switching hazards. A fabricated substrate may differ in geometry, heat flow, leakage, field concentration, material history, electrode conditions or parasitics when branch topology changes.

A real T53 comparison requires independently calibrated k0 under these conditions. It is forbidden to infer k0 equality from graph degree or R_eff alone.

No physical junction formation, new field or nonstandard constitutive law is tested in T55.
