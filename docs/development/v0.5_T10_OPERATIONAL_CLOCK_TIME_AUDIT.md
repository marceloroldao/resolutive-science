## 1. Purpose

T5 introduced a trajectory parameter lambda but explicitly prohibited identifying it with physical time. T10 asks what additional structure is required for an operational clock and whether a relational notion of time can be defined from correlations between subsystems without assuming an external absolute time.

No Hamiltonian, energy, spacetime coordinate or preferred external clock is assumed.

## 2. Ordering is weaker than time

The categorical structure already contains ordered composition:

Gamma_2 o Gamma_1.

A smooth representation may label points by lambda. Neither fact alone defines physical time.

Thus:

ORDERING != PHYSICAL_TIME
PARAMETER_LAMBDA != PHYSICAL_TIME.

## 3. Composite system and clock subsystem

Consider a represented composite system decomposed operationally as

C + S,

where C is a candidate clock subsystem and S is the subsystem whose change is described relative to C.

This decomposition is itself additional representational/physical structure. T10 does not claim a unique universal clock split.

## 4. Clock observable

Let c be an observable/readout on the clock subsystem. Along an admissible relational trajectory Gamma, a clock candidate must provide an ordered sequence of distinguishable readings

c_1, c_2, ...

or, in a continuous representation,

c(lambda).

A minimal local clock condition is monotonicity on the interval of use:

dc/dlambda != 0
with fixed sign.

Global monotonicity is not required for periodic clocks, but repeated readings then require an additional cycle counter or state information to avoid ambiguity.

## 5. Operational clock criteria

A variable is promoted from a mere parameter/readout to an operational clock only relative to a specified regime if it satisfies, to declared tolerance:

1. distinguishability — different clock states can be operationally discriminated;
2. ordered progression — readings provide a consistent local ordering;
3. repeatability/calibratability — the relation between clock states can be reproduced or calibrated;
4. correlation — changes of S can be conditioned on/read relative to C;
5. sufficient autonomy — back-action/context dependence is controlled enough for the intended description;
6. range/unambiguity — cycle count or branch information is supplied when needed.

These are operational conditions, not consequences of category theory alone.

## 6. Relational time coordinate

When c(lambda) is locally invertible, use the clock reading itself as the trajectory coordinate:

tau_R := c.

Then for a system observable q,

q(lambda) -> q(tau_R)

and

dq/dtau_R = (dq/dlambda)/(dc/dlambda).

This ratio is invariant under smooth monotonic reparameterizations of lambda because numerator and denominator acquire the same Jacobian factor.

Therefore the relational derivative with respect to a clock reading is more physical than either derivative with respect to the arbitrary parameter alone.

## 7. Main T10 derivation

For two represented observables q and c along the same trajectory,

D_c q := dq/dc
       = (dq/dlambda)/(dc/dlambda)

is invariant under orientation-preserving reparameterization lambda -> sigma(lambda), wherever dc/dlambda is nonzero.

This provides a mathematically legitimate **Resolutive Relational Rate** without an external absolute time.

Classification:

RELATIONAL_RATE = DERIVED_CONDITIONALLY
ABSOLUTE_TIME = NOT_REQUIRED.

## 8. Time as relation between states

The strongest statement currently justified is not 'time is a substance' or 'time is fundamental'. It is:

Physical temporal description can be represented relationally by correlations between changes of a chosen clock subsystem and changes of another subsystem.

Symbolically:

state relation -> clock correlation -> q(c).

This makes the earlier Resolutive intuition 'time as relation' mathematically viable, but it does not prove that all physical time is fundamentally nothing but such a relation.

## 9. Multiple clocks

Let c and k be two admissible clocks. On an interval where both are monotonic,

dk/dc = (dk/dlambda)/(dc/dlambda).

A physical theory must predict or empirically calibrate their relational rate. There is no need for either to be the absolute clock.

Clock disagreement becomes a relational observable rather than a contradiction.

## 10. Periodic clocks and topology

A periodic clock can revisit the same local reading. Therefore a phase-like clock coordinate alone is not globally injective.

A complete clock state may require

(clock phase, cycle count)

or another history/topology label.

This connects T10 to T9: topology can make local periodic readings insufficient to specify global temporal position.

No 2pi or 4pi period is selected.

## 11. Arrow/orientation audit

A monotonic clock provides a local orientation along a selected trajectory, but this does not derive a universal arrow of time.

Irreversibility, entropy production, causal asymmetry or boundary conditions would require additional dynamics/statistical structure.

Thus:

LOCAL_CLOCK_ORIENTATION = AVAILABLE
UNIVERSAL_TIME_ARROW = NOT_DERIVED.

## 12. Proper time and metric time

No metric was derived in T7. Therefore T10 does not define proper time through a line element such as

dtau^2 = -g_mu_nu dx^mu dx^nu.

A future metric theory may relate its proper-time construction to operational clocks, but that relation is not assumed here.

## 13. Time-translation symmetry

Once an operational clock tau_R is selected, one may ask whether represented dynamics are invariant under

tau_R -> tau_R + constant.

But this symmetry is an additional property of a dynamical law. T10 has not yet selected a unique dynamics.

Therefore:

TIME_TRANSLATION_SYMMETRY = NOT_DERIVED.

## 14. Generator relative to a clock

The T5 equation

dU/dlambda = G_lambda U

can be rewritten using an admissible clock:

dU/dtau_R = G_tau U,

where

G_tau = (dlambda/dtau_R) G_lambda.

The combination remains a generator of change **relative to the chosen clock**.

It is not yet energy.

## 15. Why generator is not yet energy

To identify a clock-relative generator with an energy/Hamiltonian-like quantity requires extra structure, potentially including:

- a physical state representation supporting the relevant generator notion;
- a time-translation symmetry or canonical dynamics;
- units/calibration linking the generator to energy;
- an invariant pairing/action/symplectic or quantum structure as appropriate;
- operational correspondence with measured energy.

None is supplied by T10 alone.

Thus:

T10_ENERGY = NOT_DERIVED.

## 16. Clock change covariance

If tau and tau' are two locally invertible clock coordinates related by tau'=f(tau), then

G_tau' = (dtau/dtau') G_tau.

Hence a bare numerical generator depends on clock choice, while the infinitesimal transport element

G_tau d_tau

remains invariant under reparameterization. This is consistent with T5.

A future physical energy cannot simply be identified with an arbitrary G_tau unless a physical clock normalization is selected.

## 18. Main theoretical consequence

The v0.5 ontology does not require an absolute time parameter to describe change. Once one subsystem is operationally qualified as a clock, change can be expressed through reparameterization-invariant relational rates:

D_c q = dq/dc.

Thus **Tempo Resolutivo** has a legitimate conditional definition:

> Tempo Resolutivo is the operational ordering and quantification of change of one represented subsystem relative to distinguishable change of a chosen clock subsystem.

This is a definition inside the v0.5 representation layer, not yet a claim that physical time has been fundamentally reduced or experimentally explained.

## 19. Next theoretical gate — T11: Energy and Generator Audit

T11 should now test the old intuition that energy is related to 'how fast relations change' without assuming that identification.

It must distinguish at least:

1. clock-relative generator G_tau;
2. rate of state change dq/dtau;
3. conserved generator associated with time-translation symmetry;
4. Hamiltonian in a symplectic/canonical representation;
5. quantum energy generator if a unitary complex representation is independently selected;
6. thermodynamic/internal energy.

The central question is whether any energy-like quantity follows from T1-T10 or whether energy requires an additional dynamical structure.

No formula equating energy with organization, rate, curvature, density or generator is to be assumed at the start of T11.
