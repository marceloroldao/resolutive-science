## 1. Minimal first-derivative form

Treat the four inherited coordinates collectively as

```text
R^A = (rho_R, Theta_R, iota_R, kappa_R),  A=1..4.
```

A broad local first-derivative variational family is

```text
L = 1/2 G_AB(R) g^{mu nu} partial_mu R^A partial_nu R^B - V(R),
```

up to metric/sign conventions and possible density factors.

Here `G_AB(R)` is an internal/target-space metric and `V(R)` a scalar potential.

This construction is conventional. It contains the earlier `(rho,Theta)` complex-scalar kinetic sector as a special coordinate representation but also permits infinitely many other choices.

## 2. Does the RSMS uniquely fix G_AB?

No.

The inherited four-coordinate state does not by itself determine:

- diagonal versus mixed kinetic terms;
- field-dependent versus constant coefficients;
- signature/positivity of the internal metric;
- relative normalization of rho_R, Theta_R, iota_R and kappa_R;
- whether every primitive is dynamical;
- whether constraints reduce the state space.

Therefore the variational principle alone leaves genuine model-building freedom.

## 3. Does the RSMS uniquely fix V(R)?

No.

Without an independently declared symmetry, vacuum structure, dimensional scale or interaction rule, infinitely many scalar functions `V(R)` are admissible. Setting `V=0` would itself be a model choice, not a derivation.

## 4. Can minimality select a unique action?

No.

Several inequivalent notions of minimality remain:

- fewest derivatives;
- fewest couplings;
- lowest polynomial degree;
- maximal symmetry;
- separability;
- canonical kinetic normalization.

Choosing one of these is an additional principle. The word `minimal` does not uniquely determine the physics.

## 5. Conventional-equivalence audit

If one chooses a positive-definite conventional `G_AB` and an arbitrary scalar potential, the result is a standard multi-scalar/nonlinear sigma-model class.

Thus

```text
VARIATIONAL_PRINCIPLE != RESOLUTIVE_NOVELTY.
```

Novel content would have to enter through independently derived structure in `G_AB`, `V`, constraints, connection, topology or observable mapping.
