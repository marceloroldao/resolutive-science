## 1. Purpose

T6 derived, conditionally within a multidirectional differentiable representation, a connection `A_R` and curvature `F_R`. It did not provide a metric, scalar curvature, action density, physical distance, spacetime signature or measure.

T7 asks whether a metric-like notion can be obtained from structures already available or whether an additional physical/mathematical postulate is unavoidable.

## 2. What a metric would add

On a represented base with local coordinates `x^a`, a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form would permit

```text
ds_R^2 = g^R_ab dx^a dx^b.
```

Depending on signature and interpretation this could encode local separation, norm, causal-like classification or other geometry.

But none of those interpretations is automatic.

## 3. Route M1 — Metric from mere distinguishability

The R1 ontology supplies equivalence/distinguishability classes, which provide a binary distinction:

```text
same physical class / different physical class.
```

This does not by itself provide a magnitude of difference.

Many inequivalent metrics can induce the same distinction between equal and unequal points.

Therefore:

```text
DISTINGUISHABILITY ->/-> UNIQUE_METRIC
```

and

```text
T7_M1 = INSUFFICIENT.
```

## 4. Route M2 — Metric from statistical distinguishability

If a future physical representation supplies probability distributions `p(z|x)` for neighboring represented states, one may define information-geometric quadratic forms such as a Fisher-information metric under suitable regularity assumptions.

However probability distributions, likelihoods and statistical manifolds are not present in T1-T6.

Thus:

```text
T7_M2 = VALID_FUTURE_ROUTE
PROBABILITY_STRUCTURE = NEW_ASSUMPTION
```

No Fisher metric is imported at T7.

## 5. Route M3 — Metric from symmetry

Suppose a symmetry group acts on the represented tangent space. One may seek a bilinear form invariant under that action:

```text
g(Rv,Rw)=g(v,w).
```

If the representation and symmetry are sufficiently restrictive, an invariant form may be unique up to scale. But T3 did not select a physical continuous symmetry group or tangent representation.

Therefore symmetry can constrain a metric only after additional representation data are supplied.

```text
T7_M3 = CONDITIONAL_ROUTE
UNIQUE_INVARIANT_METRIC = NOT_DERIVED
```

## 6. Route M4 — Metric from the internal generator algebra

If the local generators belong to a Lie algebra or another algebra admitting an invariant nondegenerate bilinear form `kappa`, one may define internal pairings such as

```text
kappa(A_a,A_b).
```

Examples in conventional mathematics include trace/Killing-type forms when available.

But an internal algebra pairing is not automatically a metric on the represented base. To identify

```text
g_ab ~ kappa(A_a,A_b)
```

requires a soldering/nondegeneracy assumption relating internal directions to base directions.

Therefore:

```text
INTERNAL_PAIRING != BASE_METRIC
```

without extra structure.

## 7. Route M5 — Metric as independent representation postulate

One may directly equip the represented base with

```text

```

This is mathematically valid and is the shortest route to scalar contractions, volume measures and metric geometry.

But it is not derived from the existing Resolutive ontology.

Classification:

```text
T7_M5 = VALID_MODELING_POSTULATE
T7_M5 = NOT_DERIVED
```

## 11. Measure and density

A metric on an `n`-dimensional base would conventionally permit a volume element such as

```text
dmu_g = sqrt(|det g|) d^n x
```

in coordinates.

But because the metric is not derived, neither is this measure.

More generally, a measure/density could be introduced independently of a metric.

This opens two distinct future notions:

```text
metric density
vs
independent Resolutive measure density.
```

They must not be conflated.
