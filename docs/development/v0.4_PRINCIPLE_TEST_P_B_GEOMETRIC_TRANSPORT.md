## 1. Inherited geometric facts

RSMS defines a resolutive state bundle

```text
pi_M : E_R -> M
```

with fiber modeled on the internal state space. It also admits a structure group, connection, parallel transport and curvature as mathematical structures.

However RSMS explicitly does not select a physical structure group or a connection, and states that introducing an Ehresmann connection supplies no law of motion.

Therefore the geometric vocabulary exists, but the physical transport rule is not inherited.

## 2. General transport freedom

Let gamma be a path in M and let a connection A define transport

```text
T_gamma[A] : E_{gamma(a)} -> E_{gamma(b)}.
```

For a loop, the associated holonomy may be written schematically

```text
Hol_gamma[A] = P exp(- integral_gamma A)
```

for an appropriate representation and convention.

The observable effect depends on all of the following:

- choice of structure group G_R;
- representation of G_R on the fiber;
- connection A;
- allowed path class;
- gauge equivalence;
- observable projection Pi;
- coupling of transported state to apparatus/measurement.

None of these are currently uniquely fixed by the inherited RSMS state tuple.

## 3. Can bundle geometry alone select the connection?

No.

A fiber bundle generally admits many connections. Even on a trivial product bundle, one can choose a flat connection or non-flat connections compatible with the same bundle topology.

Thus

```text
bundle structure != unique transport law.
```

Any choice of A intended to generate a specific physical effect would be an additional postulate unless derived from another principle.

## 4. Can zero curvature / flatness be selected by minimality?

Not uniquely.

Choosing

```text
F_A = 0
```

removes local curvature but is itself a physical restriction. Moreover flat connections can still have nontrivial global holonomy on non-simply-connected spaces, while trivial holonomy may require additional topology and boundary assumptions.

Conversely choosing nonzero curvature also requires an independent field equation or constitutive rule.

Therefore flatness does not emerge uniquely from the current state specification.

## 5. Can holonomy provide a new observable without new postulates?

Only conditionally.

A physically measurable holonomy requires an observable that compares transported states along inequivalent paths and is gauge invariant. A schematic discriminator is

```text
Q(gamma1,gamma2) = Pi(T_gamma1 R0) - Pi(T_gamma2 R0).
```

For this to be predictive, the following must already be frozen:

- A or its field equation;
- the representation on R;
- Pi;
- admissible paths and preparation/measurement procedure.

The current RSMS leaves these open. Therefore the existence of a possible geometric-phase observable does not make a prediction.

## 6. Relation to 4pi

A nontrivial holonomy or double cover can mathematically produce multi-step closure, but this does not rescue the previously rejected physical-4pi derivation.

To obtain a resolutive 4pi effect from transport one would have to choose a connection/topology whose holonomy realizes that behavior and then provide an observable sensitive to it. That would be a new postulate chain, not a consequence of the present primitives.

Hence

```text
GEOMETRIC_TRANSPORT does not reopen FOUR_PI_STATUS.
```

## 7. Conventional-equivalence audit

Once a connection and structure group are postulated, the framework is mathematically within standard gauge/bundle transport theory unless an independently derived resolutive constraint selects a nonstandard structure or observable.

Thus the novelty does not come from the mere presence of a bundle, connection or holonomy.
