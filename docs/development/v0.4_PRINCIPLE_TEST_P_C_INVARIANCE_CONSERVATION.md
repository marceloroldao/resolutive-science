## 1. What RSMS actually supplies

RSMS Chapter 9 provides definitions for:

- symmetry transformations;
- continuous/discrete/internal/external symmetries;
- invariant and covariant quantities;
- observable invariance;
- correspondence and reduction maps.

It explicitly states that the chapter introduces no specific physical interaction and derives no physical theory.

A specialization must declare its own symmetry group, invariants, covariance rules, correspondence regime and compatibility domain.

Therefore the framework supplies a language for constraining a model after a symmetry is selected, but does not itself select the physical symmetry.


This is standard complex-scalar field theory. It does not create Resolutive novelty and does not constrain the remaining primitive coordinates enough to select a unique Master Equation.

## 5. Could inclination/curvature define a new invariance?

The primitive coordinates `iota_R` and `kappa_R` currently lack independently derived transformation laws. Without those, one cannot define a physical group action that constrains the dynamics.

Imposing shift, reflection, rotational or scaling symmetry on these coordinates would be a model choice.

Therefore no unique conserved charge follows from their mere presence in the state tuple.

## 6. Conservation-law inversion problem

Even if one starts by demanding conservation of some scalar or current `Q`, many inequivalent dynamics can preserve the same `Q`.

Hence

```text
CONSERVATION_OF_Q ->/-> UNIQUE_EQUATION_OF_MOTION.
```

A conservation law can restrict a candidate family, but unless the conserved quantity itself is independently selected and sufficiently strong, it cannot serve as the sole selector.

## 7. Correspondence principle

Requiring reduction to established physics in an appropriate limit is scientifically important, but many extensions can share the same low-energy/classical/relativistic correspondence limit.

Therefore correspondence is a necessary compatibility gate, not a unique source of new dynamics.
